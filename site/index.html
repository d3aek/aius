01 / The problem

Six channels walk into
a pair of speakers.

Films are mixed for a room full of speakers, with dialogue living almost entirely in the center channel. When a TV or streaming stick folds that down to stereo, the center is usually attenuated and smeared under everything else. You reach for the remote, the explosions get louder, the whispers stay lost.

Center gets buried

Fixed fold-down coefficients drop the dialogue channel by ~3 dB, then pile full-level fronts and surrounds on top of it. Voices lose the fight before EQ even happens.

Surrounds smear

Ambience and music beds from the rear channels get summed straight into the front pair, masking the 2–4 kHz band where consonants (and intelligibility) live.

Black-box settings

“Clear voice” toggles are opaque, inconsistent between devices, and often just a treble boost plus heavy compression. You can't see it, tune it, or carry it to your next TV.

03 / How it works

Light in. Clarity out.

A small box between your TV's optical output and your speakers, amp or soundbar. Everything happens on a single microcontroller with no OS, no cloud, and no added lip-sync drama.

  1. 01

    Receive

    TOSLINK optical S/PDIF from the TV, stick or console. Set the source to output surround.

    S/PDIF
  2. 02

    Decode

    Each IEC 61937 burst is classified and the 5.1 bitstream unpacked into six discrete channels on the Teensy's Cortex-M7. Stereo PCM passes straight through.

    DTS Core · AC-3 · E-AC-3
  3. 03

    Matrix

    Your coefficients fold six channels into two, with dialogue given room instead of an afterthought.

    6 × 2
  4. 04

    Shape

    Per-channel and output EQ, an LFE fold with its own crossover, and a true-peak limiter on the output.

    EQ · Limiter
  5. 05

    Deliver

    Stereo back out over optical through a reset-safe gate, to the DAC, amp or soundbar you already own.

    TOSLINK out

04 / Network attached

Tune it from the couch.

aius sits on your LAN over the Teensy 4.1's native Ethernet. Open its web UI from any phone, or script it: push a new DSP configuration from a script or your home automation.

  • Built-in web UI, no app, no account
  • Local HTTP API for automation
  • Presets stored on-device
  • No cloud. Nothing phones home.
192.0.2.66
# see what's playing
$ curl http://192.0.2.66/api/status

# push a new DSP configuration
$ curl -X PUT http://192.0.2.66/api/dsp/config \
    -d @config.json

06 / Build it

Solder it. Flash it.
Hear the difference.

aius is a DIY build first. Today it runs on a hand-wired bench build: a Teensy 4.1, two TOSLINK modules and an Ethernet magjack. The carrier board's KiCad sources, BOM and manufacturing package are in the repo; no board order is authorised yet.

U1

Teensy 4.1

The brains. 600 MHz M7 with floating-point DSP headroom to spare.

J1

Ethernet kit

MagJack + header for the Teensy's built-in Ethernet PHY.

U2

TOSLINK receiver

Optical-to-electrical module feeding S/PDIF into the Teensy.

U3

Output stage

TOSLINK transmitter behind a reset-safe gate that holds it silent until the firmware is healthy.

PCB

Carrier board

Four-layer board tying it together; two layers proved infeasible for USB and Ethernet. KiCad sources included.

ENC

Enclosure

Stock Hammond extruded case; the rear panel is machined by hand or CNC.
